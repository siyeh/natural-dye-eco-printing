What Is Natural Dyeing

Natural dyeing is the process of adding colour to fibre, yarn, or fabric using dye substances found in the natural world — primarily plants, but also insects, shellfish, fungi, and certain minerals. It stands in contrast to synthetic dyeing, which uses petroleum-derived chemical dyes invented in the mid-nineteenth century. Before 1856, every dye on every piece of cloth in the world came from a natural source.

The craft is surprisingly accessible. A backyard garden, a kitchen pot, and a piece of wool or cotton are enough to get started. At the same time, natural dyeing has enormous depth: professional dyers and textile scientists spend entire careers exploring the chemistry, history, and artistic possibilities of the field.

What Can Be Dyed?

Almost any protein or plant-based fibre takes natural dye well. Wool, silk, and other animal fibres (called protein fibres) are the most receptive — they have a natural affinity for many dye molecules. Plant fibres like cotton, linen, and hemp (called cellulose fibres) can also be dyed, but usually require extra preparation. Synthetic fibres like polyester resist natural dyes almost entirely.

How Is It Different from Eco Printing?

In natural dyeing, you extract colour from a dye source into a liquid bath, and then the fibre is immersed in that bath. The plant itself is not usually in direct contact with the cloth. Step 03 — What Is Eco Printing describes the related technique in which the plant is pressed directly onto the fibre, leaving its own image behind.

Why Do People Practise It?

Reasons vary widely. Some dyers are drawn to the connection with history and place — using plants that grow locally to make colours tied to a specific landscape. Others are motivated by a desire to avoid the environmental impact of synthetic dyes. Many are simply captivated by the unpredictability and beauty of the results. Unlike synthetic dyeing, natural dyeing often produces slightly different results each time, which many practitioners find appealing rather than frustrating.
